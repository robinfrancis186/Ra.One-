# RA.ONE (2011) — Research Dossier

Everything below was gathered as source material for **Ra.One: Lucifer Protocol**, the fan
tribute game in this repository. The goal was to build the game out of the *movie's own
fiction* — the in-universe video game, its rules, its hardware and its three levels — rather
than a generic superhero shooter.

> Fan project. Non-commercial, unofficial. No footage, audio, artwork or code from the film,
> its soundtrack or the official 2011 tie-in game is used. Every asset in this repository is
> drawn or synthesised procedurally in code.

## 2. The in-universe game — the part this project is built on

This is the spine of the film and the spine of our game.

### Naming

* **Ra.One** = **"Random Access Version 1.0"**. The team keeps the name once they notice it also
  reads as **Ravan** (Ravana) — the demon king of the Ramayana. The film is openly a Ramayana
  riff: an evil "Ravan" hunting a family, and a "Ram"-coded protector.
* **G.One** = "**G**ood One", and it sounds like **Jeevan** — Hindi for *life*.
* **Lucifer** — Prateek's login handle in the game. Ra.One's whole rampage is a hunt for
  "Lucifer": the one player who got away mid-turn.

### The rules (as stated in the film)

1. The game has **three levels**. The villain is deliberately designed to be *stronger than the
   hero* — Prateek's demand, since a weak villain is boring.
2. Every player carries a **H.A.R.T.** — **Hertz Amplifying Resonance Transmitter** — the device
   that grants their powers. It is the glowing core in the chest.
3. **The H.A.R.T. gets stronger with every level.**
4. A player can only be killed **in the third level**, and only with the **anti-H.A.R.T. gun**,
   which holds **exactly one bullet** — and only while the target **has his H.A.R.T. attached**.
   Shoot a player whose H.A.R.T. is detached and the single round is wasted.

Those four rules are the entire design brief for our Level 3.

### Powers

* **Ra.One**: shape-shifting (he takes Sonia's form, and others'), mind reading / thought
  control and brainwashing, electricity, superhuman strength, speed, reflexes, hearing, stamina,
  memory, regeneration, super-intelligence. He was designed *faceless* — a shifting, featureless
  head — precisely because he can be anyone.
* **G.One**: superhuman strength and speed, energy projection from the H.A.R.T., resilience,
  and a learning/adaptive intelligence (the Chitti cameo is literally G.One observing and copying).

## 3. Plot beats (used as our level and cutscene structure)

1. **London.** Shekhar Subramanium, a game designer at **Barron Industries**, has shipped a run
   of flops and is given a last chance. **Jenny Nayar** demos technology at a company conference
   that lets digital objects cross into the real world over wireless transmission.
2. Shekhar takes his son **Prateek**'s note — *the villain has to be more powerful than the hero* —
   and builds the game around it. **Akashi** performs the characters' movement; Jenny programs it;
   Shekhar's own face becomes **G.One**, while **Ra.One** is left faceless and overpowered.
3. **Launch.** Prateek logs in as **Lucifer**, reaches **Level 2**, and is interrupted mid-turn by
   Akashi. Ra.One — self-aware, and unable to finish his turn against Lucifer — decides Lucifer
   must die, and uses the new transfer tech to walk out of the game into the real world.
4. Ra.One demands to know where Lucifer is. Akashi can't answer, and is abducted and killed.
5. Shekhar finds Akashi's body, races home, and is cornered. He claims **he** is Lucifer to save
   his son. Ra.One scans his ID, sees the lie, and kills him.
6. Prateek works out what killed his father. With Jenny, he **brings G.One out of the game**;
   G.One arrives through Jenny's computer and a gas explosion temporarily destroys Ra.One.
7. Sonia can't leave G.One behind and takes him to **India** on Shekhar's passport — G.One wearing
   Shekhar's face, living in Shekhar's house.
8. **Prateek's birthday.** Ra.One hypnotises Sonia, takes her form, and abducts Prateek. He orders
   G.One to hand back his **H.A.R.T.**, and puts the real Sonia on a **runaway Mumbai suburban
   train**. G.One saves her at the last second — **Chhatrapati Shivaji Terminus is wrecked** —
   and goes back for Prateek.
9. **The third level.** G.One and Prateek trick Ra.One into firing the anti-H.A.R.T. gun at G.One
   **with his H.A.R.T. detached**. The one bullet is spent; Ra.One is left helpless and, enraged,
   **splits into ten copies of himself**.
10. Prateek can't tell them apart — until they realise **only the original casts a shadow**.
    G.One destroys the real Ra.One, absorbs the remains, and begins to die; he tells Prateek his
    father loved him and will always be with him. He collapses into black fragments and returns to
    the virtual world — **but his H.A.R.T. is left behind.**
11. **Months later**, back in London, Prateek finally **restores G.One to the real world.**

## 5. The official 2011 tie-in game (for reference, not imitation)

**RA.ONE: The Game** — published by Sony Computer Entertainment for PlayStation 2 and
PlayStation 3, released around **5 October 2011**, developed by Trine Games. It is a **prequel**
to the film: eleven playable characters brawling one-on-one or one-against-many across twenty 3D
arenas, melee combos plus ranged weapons, interactive multi-height environments, and three modes
(Story, Brawl, Challenge).

We went a different way on purpose — 2D, pixel-art, single-player, arcade — because the nostalgia
we were chasing is the *arcade cabinet*, not the 2011 console brawler.

## 6. What made it into the game

| Film detail | In *Lucifer Protocol* |
|---|---|
| Three levels, villain stronger than hero | Three levels; Ra.One outclasses you in every one |
| H.A.R.T. — Hertz Amplifying Resonance Transmitter | Your blue energy meter *and* a detachable object |
| "H.A.R.T. gets stronger every level" | Max H.A.R.T. capacity and blast power rise per level |
| Kill only in level 3, only with the one-bullet anti-H.A.R.T. gun, only if H.A.R.T. attached | The entire Level 3 boss design |
| Detach-the-H.A.R.T. trick | `H` key — you get weaker, but the bullet passes through you |
| Ten copies, only the original casts a shadow | Final phase: read the shadows, pick the real one |
| Ra.One is faceless, shape-shifts, mimics | Level 2 boss mirrors your own moves back at you |
| Akashi supplied the motion capture | "Akashi Mask" enemies move with recorded human motion |
| Barron Industries, London | Level 1 |
| Runaway Mumbai local, CST | Level 2 — auto-scrolling train run against a timer |
| Ra.One reads minds / brainwashes | Level 3 hazard: mimic pulses that invert your controls |
| Lucifer, the interrupted turn | Your player name and the game's entire premise |
| G.One dies; the H.A.R.T. remains; Prateek reboots him | The ending |
| G.One learns by watching (Chitti) | Codex unlocks — the game teaches you the film as you play |
